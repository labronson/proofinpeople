Letters Home

When the talent couldn’t travel, the story still could.

When world events made a talent-led military campaign impossible, I asked for a day and came back with a new plan. The USO held about 150 World War II letters from service members and their families that had never been shared with the public. I brought in Legacybox to digitize them at no cost and built a co-branded archive, with Andrew Scott reading a 1944 letter.

Focus Features funded the USO’s first creator mission immersion, bringing 14 creators and media to Fort Campbell during Week of the Eagles to meet the 101st Airborne, the unit that parachuted into Normandy in the hours the film depicts.

  • Washington Post feature on release day
  • Coverage in Variety and Stars and Stripes
  • A permanent public archive and the USO’s first creator immersion

About

The same instinct,
every chapter.

I’ve always chased the work that pulls me in. The kind you can’t put down, that earns your nights and weekends not because anyone asked, but because you can’t stop thinking about it. It’s how I became Vans Warped Tour’s first intern after being told the tour didn’t take interns. It led me to My Chemical Romance, and eventually to two missions I believed in enough to give more than eight years to each.

The settings changed, but the instinct didn’t. I see what’s possible before the pieces are in place, and I connect things most people treat as separate. Then I build it, because I’ve never split strategy from execution. Done well, they’re the same job.

I’m at my best when the ambition is bigger than the resources. The strongest work finds where different interests point the same way, so everyone has a reason to make it bigger.

Underneath all of it, the work has to be true to the people it’s for. A child, a service member, a fan, a customer, a community. Never a device, always the point. That’s what turns a story into something people remember, and an organization into something people trust.
